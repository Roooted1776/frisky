How It Works

A quick walkthrough of the RedMed app and bracelet, for new users.

In short Fill out your emergency profile once in the app, write it to a passive NFC bracelet, and anyone who taps the band with a phone sees your emergency card — no app or account required on their end.

1. Set up your profile

Open the RedMed tab and tap Edit. Add your name, birth date, and blood type, then list any allergies, medications, and conditions a first responder should know. Add emergency contacts with phone numbers. Tap Save when done.

2. Write your bracelet

Bracelets ship blank. After you save your RedMed profile, go to the NFC tab (or tap PAIR BLANK BRACELET) and tap Write to NFC tag. Authenticate with Face ID, then hold the top of your iPhone near the band. Your profile is written as an NDEF URL with a #d= payload — no account and no RedMed server.

3. Verify it

Tap Scan your bracelet to read the tag and preview the same emergency card a stranger gets. Confirm your details before you rely on it.

4. What a stranger sees

Anyone who taps the bracelet opens a static read-only card in their browser: your name, blood type, allergies, conditions, medications, tap-to-call emergency contacts, Call 911, and built-in roadside first aid (bleeding, CPR, choking, shock, car crash). First-aid steps live on the page itself — not on the chip — so every band shows the same guides. No RedMed app or login is required.

5. In an emergency

Use the 911 tab for one-tap dialing and live GPS coordinates to read off to a dispatcher. Use the Aid tab for step-by-step guidance on bleeding, CPR, shock, burns, and other roadside situations while help is on the way.

6. Keeping it in sync

If you edit your profile later, reopen the NFC tab and write to the band again — the bracelet only shows what was last written to it. See our Privacy Policy for where your data lives.
