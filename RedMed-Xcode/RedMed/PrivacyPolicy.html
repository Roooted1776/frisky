Privacy Policy

Effective Date August 2026
Document Version 2.0 (United Kingdom)
Applicable law UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018

Applicable to all users in the United Kingdom of the RedMed application and the associated passive NFC medical bracelet

In Short RedMed runs no servers for your RedMed profile. There is no account and no database of your profile on our side. What you enter stays on your device and, if you choose, on your passive NFC bracelet. We do not collect it, profile you, or sell anything about you. The one thing to understand before you wear the band: anyone who taps it can read it, exactly like a printed medical ID card.

This Privacy Policy explains how RedMed handles personal data in connection with the RedMed mobile and web application (the “App”) and the associated passive NFC medical bracelet. Read it together with our Terms Of Service.

2. The Short Version of Our Position

RedMed is a tool you control — the digital equivalent of a medical ID card in your wallet. It is not a healthcare provider portal, not an NHS service, and not part of your NHS care record. Your RedMed profile is not sent to us and is not held on any RedMed server. For most of what the App does, we never become a controller of your data at all, because the data never reaches us.

3. What Data Is Involved

  • Health data (special category): allergies, medications, conditions, and blood type. Under Article 9 of the UK GDPR this is special category personal data and gets the highest level of protection.
  • Identity data: your name and date of birth.
  • Emergency contacts: names and phone numbers of people you choose to list. You should tell them you have listed them.
  • Location data: coordinates and compass heading, used only while the Find Help screen is open.

4. Our Lawful Bases

Where we do process personal data, we rely on the following:

  • Article 6(1)(b) — performance of a contract: providing the App you chose to install and use.
  • Article 6(1)(a) and Article 9(2)(a) — your consent and your explicit consent: for health data you enter and choose to write to a bracelet, and for location processing on the Find Help screen. You give consent by entering the data and by granting the operating system location permission.

You can withdraw consent at any time by clearing the data in the App, denying or revoking location permission in your device settings, and rewriting or discarding the bracelet. Withdrawing consent does not affect processing carried out before you withdrew it. Providing this data is entirely optional — the App is not usable for its purpose without a profile, but you are under no statutory or contractual obligation to provide one.

5. Where Your Data Is Held

Your profile is held on your own device and, if you write one, on your bracelet's NFC chip. It is not uploaded, synchronised, or backed up to any RedMed server, because we do not operate one. When somebody taps your band, nothing is fetched from us — the card is rendered from what is on the chip.

On iOS the profile is stored in the device Keychain, which is encrypted by the device hardware. We store it with the WhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly protection class, which means it is readable only while the device is unlocked and is excluded from iCloud and encrypted device backups — it does not sync to your other devices and does not travel with a restored backup.

6. The Bracelet Is Readable by Anyone

Your RedMed profile is written to a passive HF NFC chip at 13.56 MHz (ISO 14443 / NTAG-style). That is a different radio band from Bluetooth (~2.4 GHz): the band is not paired over Bluetooth and has no battery, sensors, or radio of its own. Your phone powers it only for a moment during an intentional tap or write — the App does not keep an active RF session with the bracelet. It does not track you, monitor you, or record anything.

The information on the chip is not encrypted and can be read by any smartphone that taps it. That is a deliberate design decision so that an ambulance clinician or a bystander needs no app, no account, and no password in an emergency. The trade-off is real: treat the band as you would a printed medical ID card, and do not put anything on it that you would not accept a stranger reading. If you would rather not disclose a particular condition, leave it out.

7. Location (Find Help)

Find Help shows your coordinates only while that screen is open, so that you can read them out to an emergency call handler. Continuous GPS starts only when the screen is visible and stops when you leave it or close the App. It is never used for advertising, profiling, or background monitoring, and your coordinates are not sent to us.

On iOS, RedMed shows an in-app location gate and may keep a suggestion banner until When-In-Use access is granted. Tapping Allow triggers the operating system's own permission dialog. Your device supplies location using GPS/GNSS, Wi‑Fi, and cellular signals as available.

RedMed is not Apple Emergency SOS via satellite, Apple Crash Detection, Android Satellite SOS, or any carrier satellite messaging service, and cannot start those features.

8. Who Else Receives Data

  • Anyone who taps your bracelet or opens a card link you have shared. This is the point of the product, and it is the main disclosure to understand.
  • Your device's operating system, when you use the dialler, the maps app, or the share sheet. Apple's or Google's own privacy terms apply to that.
  • No advertising networks, no analytics identifiers, no data brokers, and no cross-app tracking. We do not sell or share personal data for advertising, and there is no account registration.

9. Transfers Outside the UK

RedMed does not transfer your RedMed profile anywhere, because we never receive it.

Where a Google Maps Platform key is configured for the hosted web version, the Find Help screen may send coordinates to Google to look up nearby hospital names. That is a transfer to Google LLC in the United States. Any such transfer is made under the UK International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, or under the UK Extension to the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where Google is certified to it. This lookup is optional, limited to that screen, and involves coordinates only — never your RedMed profile.

10. How Long We Keep It

We keep nothing, because we receive nothing. Your data persists on your device and on your bracelet until you remove it.

To remove it from your phone, clear the fields in Edit RedMed and save — this overwrites what is stored — or delete the App, which removes its Keychain entry from the device. To remove it from the band, overwrite the chip with a blank tag or dispose of the band securely. We cannot erase a chip remotely: there is no connection between us and a bracelet, which is also why a lost band cannot be wiped from a distance. If you lose a band, treat it as you would a lost medical ID card.

Emails you send us are kept only as long as needed to deal with your query and are then deleted.

11. Your Rights

Under the UK GDPR you have the right to be informed, and rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, objection, and data portability, together with the right to withdraw consent.

Because your profile is not held on our systems, you exercise most of these rights directly and immediately on your own device: edit your profile to rectify it, clear the fields or delete the App to erase it, and rewrite or discard the band. You do not need our permission and you do not need to ask us. Where we do hold something about you — an email you have sent us, for example — write to help.RedMed@gmail.com and we will respond within one month.

Complaints. You have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office: Wycliffe House, Water Lane, Wilmslow, Cheshire SK9 5AF; helpline 0303 123 1113; ico.org.uk. We would appreciate the chance to address your concern first.

12. Automated Decision-Making

There is none. RedMed does not carry out profiling or automated decision-making producing legal or similarly significant effects. It does not score, triage, or risk-rate you. The seizure stopwatch is a timer you start yourself; it does not detect seizures or diagnose anything.

13. Cookies and Local Storage

The web version stores what you enter in your own browser so the App can work at all. Under the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 that is strictly necessary for a service you have requested, so no consent banner is required. We set no advertising, analytics, or tracking cookies.

14. Children and People You Support

A parent, guardian, or carer may create a profile for a child or for an adult they support. Whoever does so is responsible for the accuracy of that information and for the decision to use a bracelet. Where an adult lacks capacity, decisions should follow the Mental Capacity Act 2005 (or the equivalent law in Scotland or Northern Ireland). Bear in mind that a child's band is as readable as an adult's — apply the same judgement about what to put on it.

15. Security

  • Data minimisation: only what you choose to enter is stored, and only for emergency identification.
  • No transmission to us: your profile, contacts, and coordinates are not sent to RedMed.
  • Hardware-encrypted storage: on iOS the profile is held in the Keychain, unlocked-only and excluded from iCloud and backups (section 5).
  • Nothing pre-filled: the profile is empty until you enter something.
  • You can clear it: clearing the fields and saving overwrites the stored profile; deleting the App removes it from the device.
  • No background NFC scanning: the App does not continuously energise your band. Casual walk-by distance will not fire it; only a deliberate close antenna tap (~1–2 inches) reads the chip. That is HF NFC physics, not a tunable range setting. Payment / POS terminals speak a different protocol (EMV) and do not open the RedMed card. Linking is a one-shot write/verify after you tap Write or Scan, not an ongoing Bluetooth pair. A deliberate tap by another phone can still open the emergency card; that is the product.
  • Deliberately unencrypted chip: see section 6. This is the one place where we have traded confidentiality for emergency access, and we would rather say so plainly than bury it.
