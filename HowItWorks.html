How It Works

A quick walkthrough of the RedMed app and bracelet, for new users.

In short Fill out your emergency profile once in the app, write it to a passive 13.56 MHz HF NFC bracelet (not Bluetooth), and anyone who taps the band with a phone sees your emergency card — no app or account required on their end.

1. Set up your profile

Open the RedMed tab and tap Edit. Add your name, birth date, and blood type, then list any allergies, medications, and conditions a first responder should know. Add emergency contacts with phone numbers. Tap Save when done.

2. Write your bracelet

Go to the NFC tab and tap Write to NFC tag. Hold the top of your iPhone near the bracelet when prompted. Your profile is encoded directly onto the passive 13.56 MHz chip in the band — no internet connection needed. The phone does not stay paired over Bluetooth — it only powers the chip during that write (and later when someone deliberately taps). A hand at the 16″ passive standoff does not start NFC; intentional coupling is centimetre-range to the phone antenna after Write/Scan.

3. Verify it

Tap Scan your bracelet to see exactly what a stranger would see when they tap your band. Confirm your details are correct before you rely on it.

4. What a stranger sees

Anyone who taps the bracelet with their phone opens a plain, read-only emergency card in their browser: your name, blood type, allergies, medications, conditions, and tap-to-call emergency contacts, plus a one-tap Call 911 button. No RedMed app or login is required to view it.

5. In an emergency

Use the 911 tab for one-tap dialing and live GPS coordinates to read off to a dispatcher. Use the Aid tab for step-by-step guidance on bleeding, CPR, shock, burns, and other roadside situations while help is on the way.

6. Keeping it in sync

If you edit your profile later, reopen the NFC tab and write to the band again — the bracelet only shows what was last written to it. See our Privacy Policy for where your data lives.
