3. The Short Version of Our Position

RedMed is a consumer EMS assist you control — local ID + quicker Aid + help reaching professionals. Its job is to speed the handoff of self-reported patient identification to EMS and hospital staff and to support calling for help. It is not a medical device, not a health care provider portal, not an electronic medical record, and not part of any hospital or insurer system. We do not promise that using RedMed will produce any particular medical or rescue result.

Your RedMed profile is not sent to us and is not held on any RedMed server. For most of what the App does, we never receive your data at all. Read the Terms Of Service for the full medical-device, no-outcome, and liability terms.

4. What Information Is Involved

  • Patient ID / PII (self-reported): name, date of birth, blood type, allergies, medications, conditions — the fields EMS or a hospital may need quickly at the scene or on arrival.
  • Emergency contacts: names and phone numbers of people you choose to list. You should tell them you have listed them.
  • Location data: coordinates and compass heading, used only while the Find Help screen is open, so you can read them to a dispatcher.

5. How We Use Information (When We Have Any)

We do not collect your RedMed profile. Where we do handle information — for example an email you send to support — we use it only to respond to your request and to operate and improve the App’s non-profile features. We do not sell personal information, do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, and do not use it to build advertising profiles.

You control health-related fields by entering them (or leaving them blank), granting or denying location permission in iOS Settings, and writing or discarding a bracelet. Providing a profile is optional; the App’s emergency-ID purpose depends on one, but you are under no legal obligation to create one.

6. Where Your Data Is Held

Your profile is held on your own device and, if you write one, on your bracelet’s NFC chip. It is not uploaded, synchronized, or backed up to any RedMed server, because we do not operate one. When somebody taps your band, nothing is fetched from us — the card is rendered from what is on the chip.

On iOS the profile is stored in the device Keychain, which is encrypted by the device hardware. We store it with the WhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly protection class, which means it is readable only while the device is unlocked and is excluded from iCloud and encrypted device backups — it does not sync to your other devices and does not travel with a restored backup.

7. The Bracelet Is Readable by Anyone

Your RedMed profile is written to a passive HF NFC chip at 13.56 MHz (ISO 14443 / NTAG-style). That is a different radio band from Bluetooth (~2.4 GHz): the band is not paired over Bluetooth and has no battery, sensors, or radio of its own. Your phone powers it only for a moment during an intentional tap or write — the App does not keep an active RF session with the bracelet. It does not track you, monitor you, or record anything.

The information on the chip is not encrypted and can be read by any smartphone that taps it. That is a deliberate design decision so that an EMS clinician or a bystander needs no app, no account, and no password in an emergency. The trade-off is real: treat the band as you would a printed medical ID card, and do not put anything on it that you would not accept a stranger reading. If you would rather not disclose a particular condition, leave it out.

8. Location (Find Help)

Find Help shows your coordinates only while that screen is open, so that you can read them out to a 911 dispatcher. Continuous GPS starts only when the screen is visible and stops when you leave it or close the App. It is never used for advertising, profiling, or background monitoring, and your coordinates are not sent to us.

On iOS, RedMed shows an in-app location gate and may keep a suggestion banner until When-In-Use access is granted. Tapping Allow triggers the operating system’s own permission dialog. Your device supplies location using GPS/GNSS, Wi‑Fi, and cellular signals as available.

RedMed is not Apple Emergency SOS via satellite, Apple Crash Detection, or any carrier satellite messaging service, and cannot start or control those radios. Find Help’s Call button opens the system Phone app to your regional public emergency number only — RedMed does not attach name, medical fields, contacts, GPS, or any other PII/PHI to that call. If your carrier has enabled Starlink Direct-to-Cell (or similar), the handset may carry the call over satellite when towers are down — that path is between your phone, your carrier, and public safety. RedMed uses no Bluetooth, operates no servers for your profile, and writes the band as passive HF NFC only.

9. Who Else Receives Data

  • EMS, hospital staff, or anyone who taps your bracelet (or opens a card link you have shared) can read the self-reported patient ID / PII on the chip or card. That local handoff is the product. RedMed does not push the profile into a hospital EHR for you.
  • Your device’s operating system, when you use the Phone app, Maps, Messages, or the share sheet. Apple’s privacy terms apply to that. Public emergency services you dial or text receive whatever you say or type in that system sheet — not a RedMed server copy of your profile.
  • No advertising networks, no analytics identifiers, no data brokers, and no cross-app tracking. We do not sell or share personal information for advertising, and there is no account registration.

10. Third-Party Maps Lookups

RedMed does not transfer your RedMed profile anywhere, because we never receive it.

Where a Google Maps Platform key is configured for the hosted web version, the Find Help screen may send coordinates to Google to look up nearby hospital names. That request goes to Google LLC. It is optional, limited to that screen, and involves coordinates only — never your RedMed profile. Google’s terms and privacy policy apply to that lookup.

11. How Long Information Is Kept

We keep nothing of your RedMed profile, because we receive nothing. Your data persists on your device and on your bracelet until you remove it.

To remove it from your phone, clear the fields in Edit RedMed and save — this overwrites what is stored — or delete the App, which removes its Keychain entry from the device. To remove it from the band, overwrite the chip with a blank tag or dispose of the band securely. We cannot erase a chip remotely: there is no connection between us and a bracelet, which is also why a lost band cannot be wiped from a distance. If you lose a band, treat it as you would a lost medical ID card.

Emails you send us are kept only as long as needed to deal with your query and are then deleted.

16. Security

  • Data minimization: only what you choose to enter is stored, and only for emergency identification.
  • No transmission to us: your profile, contacts, and coordinates are not sent to RedMed.
  • Hardware-encrypted storage: on iOS the profile is held in the Keychain, unlocked-only and excluded from iCloud and backups (section 6).
  • Nothing pre-filled: the profile is empty until you enter something.
  • You can clear it: clearing the fields and saving overwrites the stored profile; deleting the App removes it from the device.
  • No background NFC scanning: the App does not continuously energize your band. Casual walk-by distance will not fire it; only a deliberate close antenna tap (~1–2 inches) reads the chip. Payment / POS terminals speak a different protocol (EMV) and do not open the RedMed card.
  • Deliberately unencrypted chip: see section 7. This is the one place where we have traded confidentiality for emergency access, and we would rather say so plainly than bury it. See also Security.
